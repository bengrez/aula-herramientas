The rock cycle

Rocks are not forever. Over millions of years, every rock on Earth can be broken down, buried, squeezed, melted and made into a new rock.

01 · Rocks and minerals

A rock is a mixture of minerals

Mineral

A naturally occurring solid with a fixed chemical make-up and a regular crystal structure.

quartzfeldsparmicacalcite

Rock

A solid made of one or more minerals (or bits of other rocks) stuck together.

Granite = quartz (grey, glassy) + feldspar (pink/white) + mica (black, shiny).

Geologists sort rocks by how they formed:

Igneous

From molten rock that cooled and hardened.

Latin ignis = fire

Sedimentary

From sediments (bits of rock, sand, shells) pressed and glued together.

Latin sedimentum = settling

Metamorphic

From an existing rock changed by heat and pressure, without melting.

Greek meta + morphē = change of form
NotasAnalogía que funciona: los minerales son los ingredientes y la roca es la galleta. Fijar los colores de aquí al final: rojo ígneas, amarillo sedimentarias, morado metamórficas. Que diga con sus palabras qué significa cada nombre antes de avanzar.

02 · Igneous rocks

Cooling speed decides the crystal size

Magma is molten rock underground. When it reaches the surface it is called lava. As it cools, minerals crystallise.

IntrusiveExtrusive

Ejemplo cotidiano: el azúcar que cristaliza lento hace cristales grandes (azúcar cande); la que se enfría de golpe queda como caramelo vidrioso, igual que la obsidiana. La pómez tiene agujeros porque la lava tenía gas y se enfrió tan rápido que las burbujas quedaron atrapadas.

03 · Sedimentary rocks

Five steps from mountain to sandstone

1. WeatheringRock breaks into pieces (rain, ice, roots, heat)
→
2. ErosionWater, wind or ice carry the pieces away
→
3. DepositionPieces settle in layers, usually under water
→
4. CompactionNew layers on top squeeze out water
→
5. CementationDissolved minerals glue the grains together

Examples

sandstone (sand) · shale (mud) · conglomerate (pebbles) · limestone (shells and calcite)

Clues it is sedimentary

Visible layers · rounded grains · fossils. It is the only rock family that usually keeps fossils.

NotasRevelar paso a paso y que ella diga el siguiente antes de hacer clic. Diferencia clave que se confunde: weathering rompe la roca en su lugar; erosion la transporta. Ejemplo local: la arena de las playas de Perth es cuarzo erosionado de rocas antiguas, y las Pinnacles (Nambung) son pilares de caliza (Tamala Limestone) hecha de arena con conchas cementadas. Pregunta: ¿por qué las metamórficas e ígneas casi nunca tienen fósiles? (el calor y la fusión los destruyen).

04 · Metamorphic rocks

Cooked and squeezed, but never melted

Deep underground, or next to hot magma, existing rock is changed by heat and pressure. Minerals re-form and line up. If it melted, it would become magma instead.

NotasVolver a la pregunta de entrada (la arena de Cottesloe): granito → weathering y erosion → sedimentos. Pedirle que recorra un camino completo en voz alta, por ejemplo magma → granito → sedimentos → arenisca → cuarcita → magma. El motor del ciclo es doble: el calor interno de la Tierra (tectónica, volcanes) y la energía del Sol (clima, agua, viento).

Summary

The rock cycle in six lines

  1. Igneous rocks form when magma or lava cools. Slow cooling (underground) = large crystals; fast cooling (surface) = small crystals or glass.
  2. Sedimentary rocks form by weathering → erosion → deposition → compaction → cementation. Look for layers and fossils.
  3. Metamorphic rocks form when heat and pressure change an existing rock without melting it.
  4. Any rock can become any other: there are many paths around the cycle, not one circle.
  5. Most processes take thousands to millions of years; eruptions and landslides are fast.
  6. How a rock formed gives it its properties, and its properties decide its uses.
